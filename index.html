DYNAMICS / REAL SELECTION CAPACITY

One frozen chain, two travel directions

With an ideal hand-set bidirectional chain, cues at opposite ends produce exact four-beat sequences in 62.5% and 67.7% of 96 paired trials per direction. Shuffling the ideal matrix largely removes the effect. The original six-unit dynamics can express both orders without a separate context neuron—but this does not mean the colony learned those links.

LEARNING / THE MISSING STATE-DEPENDENT RULE

A timer counter can learn adjacency, not yet self-teach

An engineered nearest-predecessor counter extracts adjacent connections from externally forced orders and replays them with fixed beat taps at 68.8% / 80.2%. It only works inside its hand-chosen 1–5 tick credit window; learned tap filters do not beat fixed taps 0–1. The weight matrix could be reconstructed from the teaching schedule without running the oscillators.

CONTROL / FAST STATE VERSUS SLOW MEMORY

Last lesson changes what replays by itself

Changing only the final teaching order switches no-cue replay: 0123 rises 3% → 31%, while 3210 falls 32% → 5%. Some apparent cue success at gaps where nothing was learned is just residual timing. Remaining problem: a learning rule where the units’ own state determines credit and builds useful routing.

Source-backed scripts and results: sixth-round check · fifth-round data · interactive colony. This is a synthetic simulator, not biological synapses, task-general AI, or demonstrated MIT theta cognition.

NOVEMBER 10, 2026 · CAUSAL LENS / SOURCE-REPORTED FOLLOW-UP

The ECG-like loop beats when it forgets — but it is not a neuron

The accidental checkerboard pulse returned to the laboratory. The now-published forgetting-clock experiment shows a refractory-like mechanism: large readings keep a 50-sample controller quiet until they fall out of the window, then delayed feedback triggers another beat. Its synapse-like motif is history-dependent responsiveness, not a biological synapse or learned plasticity.

Read the follow-up and evidence limits ↗
CLOCK / REPORTED EXPLANATION

Fifty ticks of quiet, then a reset

The proposed 59-tick rhythm decomposes as 50 ticks of ordered recent history, one threshold recognition tick and two four-tick journeys through the feedback loop. The zero in a blind sensor region still matters because it differs from the setpoint.

Follow-up claims a 204-setting sweep with 18 approximate period doublings. The experiment scripts and numerical results are now committed in GeometricNeuronOriginReview; Genealogy has not independently rerun the full sweep. This is a bounded measured mechanism, not a universal theorem.

PHASE / REPORTED INTERVENTIONS

Insensitive to tiny pushes, resettable by large ones

Fable reports one sampled 59-tick attractor from 3,000 starts and kicks at all 59 phases: changes up to 0.03 caused no lasting shift; stronger positive kicks shifted timing, while a −1 kick commonly restarted the clock.

No proof of a unique global attractor, no independently inspected scripts, and no demonstrated memory beyond a potentially readable phase. Zero local gradient is not zero finite causal influence.

LENS / SCIENTIFIC CORRECTION

More pixels can give a worse causal explanation

The published reduction showed the entire image path can become one scalar lookup without changing the waveform. Changing image-vector dimension altered the feedback sensor's footprint and the pulse itself: measurement was part of the dynamics.

This is an excitable software feedback controller, not an ion-channel cell, synapse, or hidden eigenmode machine. The deeper lens is the intervention that distinguishes cause from resemblance.
