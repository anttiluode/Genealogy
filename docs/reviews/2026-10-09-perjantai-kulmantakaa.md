## What Perjantai establishes

Perjantai places camera memory, current belief and a next-frame prediction on
three refracting moons. A shared least-squares filter uses image neighbourhoods,
motion, a running average and bias; optional phase features extend the twenty
linear coefficients by two. The attention display follows running error energy.
That is a programmed change detector, not a learned information-gain policy.

The upstream core checks and round-2 evaluation were independently rerun for this
atlas pass. The synthetic precision benefit survives, while the predeclared
smooth-motion phase gain fails. Pixel-local phase does not earn object
permanence behind the synthetic bar. Duplicate frames hurt prediction but do
not satisfy the original collapse diagnosis. Fast-motion phase gains reported
after the failed primary gate remain exploratory and were not rerun here.

The owner's assessment that the follow-up does not deliver the excitement of
AnttisBrain2 is retained as a design outcome, not turned into a failed scientific
metric. A correct prediction benchmark and an engaging demonstration are separate
goals. No recorded real-camera dataset or broad model comparison is supplied.

## What KulmanTakaa is proposing

The intended instrument observes a floor or other light surface beside an
occluding edge. It first has to isolate a small spatially structured change and
then determine whether its angular structure identifies motion on the hidden
side. Background subtraction and inverse geometry are distinct steps.

The proposed bench can start with an emitting phone screen behind a book or
cardboard edge. That is an easier calibration task than recovering naturally
reflected light from a person in another room. A deliberate pinhole and an edge
are also different operators and must be calibrated separately.

Prior art is explicit: Bouman et al.,
[Turning Corners into Cameras: Principles and Methods](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Bouman_Turning_Corners_Into_ICCV_2017_paper.html),
ICCV 2017, reconstructs a one-dimensional hidden-motion video from standard RGB
recordings beside an edge. Varjoluotain instead ports the 2019 known-occluder
screen reconstruction. Neither result establishes this planned browser's
performance. No absence-of-competing-apps or guaranteed-phone-compatibility
claim is made.

## A result that would count

| Check | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Input boundary | The decoder receives only the camera region beside the edge; actor position, animation phase, labels and future frames stay outside it. |
| Physical occlusion | The actor is actually hidden, including reflections or leaked screen edges; an open-view recording is a separate reference. |
| Independent recording | Freeze the calibration and parameters before shuffled held-out recordings with separately recorded ground truth. |
| Optical ablation | Cover or alter the relevant light path while preserving nuisance brightness where possible; compare with a spatially uninformative region. |
| Negative controls | Empty hidden scene, global illumination changes, visible-side motion and camera exposure/gain changes. |
| Useful recovery | Recover direction or distinguish independently varied trajectories beyond a total-brightness or generic motion detector. |
| Simulation boundary | A bench generated by the decoder's own forward model checks implementation; it does not count as a real optical demonstration. |

At the reviewed initial KulmanTakaa snapshot there are no implementation files,
test results or outcome receipts. The atlas therefore adds an active proposal
and an open discriminator, with no empirical success record for KulmanTakaa.
